# Storyboard: Don't Knock, He's Busy (Judges 3)

A rowdy late-70s Southern country-funk story song (about 109 BPM, chicken-pickin' guitar, rubbery
bass, stop-time hits before the punchlines, a big call-and-response chorus), told as a **dark biblical
papercraft**: the whole world a handmade miniature of torn cardstock, parchment, pulp, black card,
tarnished foil, thin wood, thread and wax, shot like a stop-motion feature by candle, torch and
moonlight. The song is wry and funny; the picture keeps the danger underneath: deep shadows, real
weight, mischief in the timing (puppets move on the stop-time hits, freeze on the punchlines).

The story (Judges 3:12-30): Moab has ruled Israel eighteen years. Ehud, a left-handed Benjamite,
brings the tribute to Eglon king of Moab, a very fat man, in his cool upper (summer) room at the
palace. Ehud has a double-edged dagger a cubit long strapped on his right thigh under his clothes. He
sends the bearers home, turns back at the carved stones (the quarries) by Gilgal, and asks for a
private word. Eglon sends everyone out and rises; Ehud says he has a message from God and thrusts the
dagger in with his left hand. He slips out through the porch, locks the doors and goes. The servants
find the doors locked and think the king is relieving himself; they wait until they are ashamed, then
fetch the key and find their lord dead on the floor. Ehud escapes past the quarries to Seirah, blows
the ram's horn in the hill country of Ephraim, Israel comes down after him, they seize the fords of
the Jordan against Moab, and the land rests eighty years.

Ehud is God's deliverer: never a villain, never gleeful about death. The killing is shown only as
**shadow theatre** (silhouettes on a lit parchment screen) with dried-blood crimson ink, no gore,
nothing anatomical. Eglon is rotund and vain but still a king: comic, never grotesque. God is never
shown. People are articulated papercraft puppets in profile (lib/puppet.js): Ehud a young man (style
2) in a rust tunic with a short beard; Eglon (style 4) in purple-black and gold; guards (style 5) in
bronze helmets; Israelites in earth tones (styles 0 to 3).

Words: the lyric layer is drawn separately (scenes/*.lyric.js) as torn parchment strips with
letterpress ink and wood-type placards. Keep each scene's **words zone** dark and calm: no light
source, no bright paper, no busy detail there.
